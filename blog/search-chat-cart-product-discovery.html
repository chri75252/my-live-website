From Search to Chat to Cart: How Product Discovery Is Changing

Product discovery is becoming distributed across search, AI assistants, social content, creators, images and embedded shopping experiences.

Published 2026-07-27Updated 2026-07-27Publisher: The Blacksmith Market Ltd

Key takeaway: Search engines remain important, but customers increasingly begin with AI assistants, social feeds, creators, marketplaces, live video or images. Businesses need one trustworthy information foundation that can support all of those routes.

Traditional ecommerce planning often assumes that a customer already knows what to type. That model remains useful for explicit demand such as a product model, category or replacement part. Other journeys begin with a situation, a creator demonstration, a visual reference or a broad question. The customer may not yet know the product name.

The practical consequence is not that conventional search is disappearing. It is that intent can now be created, refined and converted across more surfaces.

Five discovery modes

1. Intent-led search

The customer expresses a specific need using keywords. Crawlable pages, useful category information, accurate titles, internal links and clear structured data still matter. Google’s current guidance states that the same core SEO practices apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode; it does not require a special AI schema or separate AI text file.

2. Conversational discovery

The customer describes a situation, constraints or desired result. Useful content needs to explain who an option is for, how alternatives differ, what limitations apply and which facts are current. OpenAI’s publisher guidance separately explains that public sites seeking visibility in ChatGPT Search should not block OAI-SearchBot.

3. Feed and creator discovery

A customer encounters a product through content rather than a deliberate search. TikTok describes this behaviour as discovery ecommerce. Demonstration, creator fit and trust can matter more than a conventional product-title keyword, but viral reach is not the same as repeatable channel economics.

4. Visual and multimodal discovery

A customer starts with an image, screenshot or a combination of text and image. Shopify’s 2026 developer material highlights image and multimodal search capabilities in its commerce infrastructure. Businesses need strong images and complete attributes—colour, size, material, dimensions, compatibility and style—to support this route.

5. Embedded and agentic discovery

Commerce can appear inside another activity such as travel planning, messaging, entertainment or an AI assistant. Contextual commerce depends on reliable catalogue data, current inventory, permissioned checkout and clear attribution.

The website still matters

Distributed discovery does not make the owned website irrelevant. It remains the most controllable place to establish identity, explain products or commercial opportunities, publish policies, correct information and convert referrals. Its role changes from being the only storefront to being an authoritative source and owned experience.

Editorial context and product data work together

Editorial content explains problems, use cases, comparisons and limitations. Product data provides titles, attributes, variations, price, availability, delivery, policies and images. One creates understanding; the other allows platforms and assistants to identify or act on the offer accurately.

A channel-readiness framework

QuestionWhy it matters
Is the relevant audience present?Large platform reach does not guarantee commercial fit.
Does the product demonstrate well?Social, creator and video discovery favour visible outcomes.
Is product information complete?AI, marketplaces and visual search depend on reliable attributes.
Can operations support uneven demand?Discovery-led channels can produce sudden demand spikes.
Who owns the customer relationship?Platforms vary in data access, communication and checkout control.
Can performance be attributed?Discovery, assisted conversion and direct checkout need different measures.
Are returns and support ready?Fast discovery can create mismatched expectations if content is incomplete.

What businesses should do now

  1. Keep normal SEO strong. Important content should remain in accessible HTML with coherent internal links.
  2. Write for situations, not only keywords. Explain use cases, fit, alternatives and limitations.
  3. Build reusable product information. Use consistent categories and attributes across sites, feeds and platforms.
  4. Produce demonstrable content. Use original images, diagrams, comparisons and video where useful.
  5. Connect discovery to operations. Do not launch a channel that inventory, fulfilment and returns cannot support.
  6. Measure the whole journey. Track source, assisted conversion, return rate, repeat purchase and contribution margin—not traffic alone.

Avoid false choices

The decision is rarely SEO or social, website or AI assistant, marketplace or owned channel. A practical strategy maintains one trustworthy operating foundation and adapts its presentation to each relevant surface.

Conclusion

Product discovery is becoming a network rather than one funnel. Businesses do not need to chase every new surface, but they do need information that is accurate, portable and useful wherever discovery begins.
