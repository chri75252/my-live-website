Search and AI discovery

Make the website understandable before adding AI-specific files.

A durable foundation combines consistent URLs, accurate crawl instructions, truthful identity signals and content with original value.

Published by The Blacksmith Market Ltd · Published 27 July 2026 · Updated 27 July 2026

Search engines and answer systems do not need a website to use a special writing style. They do need consistent URLs, accessible pages, reliable identity signals and content that is worth retrieving.

1. Establish one canonical domain

Choose one HTTPS hostname and make the sitemap, canonical tags, Open Graph URLs, internal links and structured data agree. Google recommends using consistent canonical signals and linking internally to the canonical URL. See Google Search Central’s canonical URL guidance.

2. Publish an accurate sitemap

A sitemap should contain only canonical, indexable pages that actually exist. Google’s sitemap documentation recommends fully qualified URLs and consistent canonical choices. The presence of a sitemap is not a pass when it lists obsolete or missing URLs.

3. Keep important content in normal HTML

Page titles, headings, explanations, authorship and key facts should be available without requiring a user interaction. JavaScript can enhance the experience, but Google’s guidance for AI features still centres on crawlability, indexability and eligibility to appear with a normal Search snippet. See AI features and your website.

4. Remove credibility shortcuts

Invented authors, testimonials, addresses, ratings and operating statistics create avoidable trust and accuracy risks. Public company records, third-party databases and the website itself should not contradict one another.

5. Use structured data as a description, not decoration

Organization, WebSite, BlogPosting and breadcrumb data can clarify entities and relationships. Google requires structured data to represent the visible page accurately and not mislead users. See the structured data policies.

6. Create non-commodity content

Useful content contributes tested methods, original examples, calculations, data or experienced judgement. Generic summaries are less likely to provide a strong reason for search systems, publishers or readers to select the page over established alternatives.

7. Treat AI-specific files as optional

A clear robots.txt, canonical structure and substantive content matter more than optional conventions such as llms.txt. Google states that no special AI text file or schema is required for its generative Search features. The llms.txt proposal is a voluntary convention, not a universal ranking or ingestion standard.

8. Measure conventional and AI discovery

Use Search Console’s Performance report for queries, pages, impressions, clicks and average position. Track referral traffic from answer systems separately and review which pages are cited where platform reporting is available.

Implementation checklist

  • One canonical HTTPS hostname
  • Correct robots and sitemap references
  • No broken editorial links or fake archive controls
  • Truthful entity and contact information
  • Unique titles and descriptions
  • Visible authorship and publication/update dates
  • Valid structured data matching visible content
  • Original content with sources and limitations
  • Automated link and canonical validation

This checklist describes eligibility and good practice. It cannot guarantee indexing, ranking or citation by any search or AI system.
